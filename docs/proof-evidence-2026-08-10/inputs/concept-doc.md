## Pressure support — patient does the work, machine just helps

This is the mode where the patient triggers every single breath themselves and the ventilator just adds a boost of pressure on top. Higher pressure support = bigger breaths, which makes sense once you think about it as "the machine assisting, not driving." Need to remember this is different from AC where the machine can force a breath.

## PEEP — the pressure that's always left over

PEEP = positive end-expiratory pressure — it's the pressure that never goes fully back to zero at the end of a breath, even during exhale. The number he used as an example was 5. The reason it matters: it keeps the little air sacs (alveoli) from collapsing between breaths, which is a big deal for oxygenation.

## FiO2 — how much oxygen you're actually giving

FiO2 is just the fraction of the air the patient is breathing that's actually oxygen (vs. room air's ~21%). In his example he set it to 50%. This is the number I'd expect to see change the most depending on how sick someone's lungs are.
